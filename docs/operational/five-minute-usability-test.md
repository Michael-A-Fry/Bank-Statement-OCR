# The five-minute usability test

A script for watching one accountant convert one real case, unaided, and writing
down where the screen made them stop. Run it whenever the Convert screen changes,
and with every new person who joins the team (their first five minutes are the
test nobody else can do twice).

## What you need

- One accountant who has **not** used Statement Studio before, or has not used
  it since the last change. Not the person who asked for the change.
- One real case folder from their own work: 4 to 10 statements, ideally with at
  least one that is a photo or scan, one from a bank account they have converted
  before, and one CSV. Real files only - the point is what *their* statements do.
- A laptop at their desk with Statement Studio open on the **Convert** tab.
- A timer, and this page printed (or open on your phone, not their screen).

Ask before you start: "Is it all right if I watch and take notes? I am testing
the tool, not you." Nothing they say or do is recorded anywhere but your notes;
write no client names, account numbers or amounts down.

## The one instruction

Read this out, word for word, then stop talking:

> "Please turn this case folder into spreadsheets you would be happy to use. Do
> it the way you would on your own. I can't help - but please say out loud what
> you are thinking as you go."

Start the timer. **Do not help.** If they ask you a question, say "What would you
do if I weren't here?" If they are stuck for more than 60 seconds, write down
exactly where, then say "Let's move on" and show them that one step.

## What to watch for (tick as you see it)

| # | Moment | Good sign | Write down if not |
|---|--------|-----------|-------------------|
| 1 | Getting the files in | Drags the folder onto the page, or uses Browse, within 30 s | Where they looked first |
| 2 | The bank column | Leaves banks the tool filled in; notices "Same bank as last time" | Any bank they changed, and why |
| 3 | Pressing Convert | Finds the one green button without searching | What they tried to click first |
| 4 | The case summary | Reads the "what happens now" line and acts on it | What they thought "Needs you" meant |
| 5 | Next file to check | Uses it to walk the files that need them | Whether they went row by row instead |
| 6 | A file that needs them | Understands the question in one read (e.g. "Two columns could be money out - tell us which") | The words they re-read, or asked about |
| 7 | It's right / Set aside | Chooses with confidence; notices the 10-second Undo | Any surprise at what happened next |
| 8 | A file that couldn't be read | Follows the next step it gives (e.g. "try the original PDF from the bank") | What they did instead |
| 9 | Downloading | Finds Download everything; opens a workbook and trusts it | Any number they went to check by hand |

## When they say they are done

Stop the timer. Ask three questions, and write the answers in their words:

1. "Is there anything in those spreadsheets you would check again before using
   them?"
2. "Was there a moment you were not sure what to do?"
3. "If you could change one thing on that screen, what would it be?"

## Scoring

- **Pass**: the whole case done in five minutes or less, no help given, and no
  answer to question 1 that points at the tool's figures.
- **Needs work**: any step above where they stopped for 60 seconds, any help
  given, or any word they asked the meaning of.

Every "needs work" becomes one line in the team's improvement list, quoting the
moment ("Stopped at the Outcome column - asked what 'Read as' meant"). Fix the
words first: in Statement Studio nearly every stop so far has been a word.

## What this test never changes

The arithmetic proof, the "always ask once" check of a new statement design, and
what is recorded about who checked a file are not usability questions. If a
person finds them slow, make the question clearer - never skip it.
